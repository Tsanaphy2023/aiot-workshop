Workshop Network Setup

IoT in Smart Farming workshop · Last updated 2026-09-22

This document was compiled with Claude AI. Its content was designed and reviewed by those responsible, who certify that it reflects the intent of the preparing team in every respect.

Supersedes NETWORK-REQUIREMENTS.md (the per-SSID VLAN design), retired 21 Sep 2026. That document and its Thai edition stay in the repository for reference only.

The workshop runs two separate wireless networks in one room:

  1. The workshop LAN — three GL.iNet Flint routers we bring and configure ourselves, one per zone (red / green / blue). Each carries that zone's boards on 2.4 GHz and that zone's Home Assistant laptop and one phone per team on 5 GHz. This is where boards, Home Assistant and dashboards find each other by hostname (mDNS). No VLANs, no controller, no coordination with anyone: each Flint is its own subnet, so zone isolation is a property of the hardware.
  2. The internet WiFi — provided by NT, 5 GHz only, for every other laptop and phone in the room. Its job is internet access for the worksheet site and web.esphome.io. Devices on it cannot reach any board or Home Assistant, and that is by design.

The three Flints plug into NT's gateway by cable for their own internet access (web.esphome.io from the Home Assistant laptop; the Session 3 remote-access demo).

Section 1 is what we ask of NT. Sections 2–5 are our own configuration, the rules for participants, the acceptance test and the risks. Section 6 records why this replaced the VLAN design.

1. What we ask of NT

NT is the venue's internet provider for this event, contracted for 500 devices. Nothing below asks NT to configure device-to-device traffic, multicast or VLANs; those all live inside our routers.

1.1 Three wired ports for our routers

Three RJ45 Ethernet ports, 1 GbE, each giving a DHCP lease with plain internet access. Each port feeds one of our routers, which NATs a zone of roughly 170 clients behind a single MAC and IP. On these three ports: no captive portal, no MAC registration, no login of any kind, no per-MAC bandwidth cap that treats the router as one user, and no idle timeout. Isolation between the three ports is fine; they never need to see each other.

The ports can be at one location, in which case we run our own cables (up to ~50 m) to the three zone positions, or one near each zone position. Bandwidth need is modest, under 50 Mbps per port. A fourth port of the same kind for the presentation laptop, near the stage, if convenient.

Outbound traffic: unrestricted is preferred. If NT filters, then at minimum TCP 443, UDP 53 and UDP 123 from all four ports. No inbound access is required anywhere.

1.2 The internet WiFi

5 GHz only. 2.4 GHz off on every NT access point. Our three routers are the only 2.4 GHz transmitters in the room; 200 microcontroller boards live there and they are the clients that cannot cope with interference.

  • Capacity: up to ~600 associations — 300 laptops plus up to 300 phones, since each team's remaining three members join NT rather than our routers. The contract says 500; the difference is the phones people may not join.
  • SSID name that says what it is, for instance farm-internet. Participants will see two kinds of WiFi and must pick the right one from the name alone. Please do not name it with farm-iot or farm-laptop; those are ours.
  • Login: preferably none. If a captive portal is required, one login per device for the whole two days, no daily re-authentication.
  • Client isolation on NT's WiFi is fine, and even preferable: nothing on it talks to anything else on it.

1.3 Sharing the 5 GHz band

Our routers and NT's access points share 5 GHz in one ballroom. We keep to three narrow, non-DFS channels so NT keeps the rest of the band:

Our routers use 5 GHz channels 36, 44 and 149, each at 20 MHz. Please keep NT's 5 GHz radios off those three channels, or tell us which channel plan NT prefers and we will move. Everything else, including all DFS channels (52–144) and 157–165, is NT's.

Please do not mount an NT access point directly above or beside one of our routers; a few metres apart is enough.

1.4 Setup window and support

We ask for the three ports to be live during the setup window on the afternoon before Day 1, with someone on call who can change the NT configuration, and on-site or on-call support through the first hour of the workshop. The acceptance test in section 4 is what we will run.

2. Our routers: configuration per zone

Three GL.iNet Flint routers (Flint 2, GL-MT6000, or Flint 3, GL-BE9300; settings are the same on both), plus a fourth as a cold spare carrying all three zone configurations exported. Configure all four in advance, at the office, from a single checklist; the only site work is plugging in.

2.1 SSIDs, subnets, channels

Zone 2.4 GHz SSID (boards) 5 GHz SSID (HA laptop, one phone) LAN subnet Router IP 2.4 GHz ch. 5 GHz ch.
red farm-iot-red farm-laptop-red 10.10.0.0/22 10.10.0.1 1 36
green farm-iot-green farm-laptop-green 10.20.0.0/22 10.20.0.1 6 44
blue farm-iot-blue farm-laptop-blue 10.30.0.0/22 10.30.0.1 11 149

Password iotfarmer on all six SSIDs. WPA2-PSK (AES/CCMP), not WPA2/WPA3 mixed.

The SSID names and the password are compiled into the firmware of 200 boards and cannot be changed on site. They are unchanged from the VLAN design, so no firmware rebuild is needed.

Why a /22 and not the router's default 192.168.8.0/24: a /24 is 250 leases, and one zone needs ~170–270 (67 boards, 34 laptops × 2 for the bridged VM, 34 phones, spares). Distinct subnets per zone also make a wrong-zone device obvious from its address.

2.2 Settings checklist, every router

Work through the GL.iNet admin UI (http://<router IP>) and, where noted, LuCI (System → Advanced Settings).

Wireless

  • [ ] 2.4 GHz: SSID and password per the table; WPA2-PSK; fixed channel per the table; 20 MHz bandwidth; TX power maximum; mode left at default (b/g/n/ax mixed). If boards fail to associate on the bench, force 802.11n in LuCI.
  • [ ] 5 GHz: SSID and password per the table; WPA2-PSK; fixed channel per the table; 20 MHz; TX power maximum. Never a DFS channel (52–144): a radar event vacates the channel for a minute or more and the whole zone drops.
  • [ ] Different SSID per band. Do not use "same SSID on both bands" / band steering.
  • [ ] Guest WiFi off on both bands (the guest network is the only place client isolation exists on these routers; the main network has none).
  • [ ] PMF / 802.11w: optional or disabled, never required. 802.11r off.
  • [ ] 6 GHz radio (Flint 3 only): off. Nothing in the room uses it and it is one more thing to explain.

LAN and DHCP

  • [ ] LAN IP and mask per the table.
  • [ ] DHCP pool: the whole /22 minus the first 20 addresses. Lease time 72 hours, covering both days plus the setup window. Reservations are not needed; everything is addressed by hostname.
  • [ ] Confirm in LuCI that IGMP snooping is off on br-lan (OpenWrt default; check it stayed that way).
  • [ ] The router's DNS resolves DHCP client hostnames, so http://gogo-iot-red-6749a4/ works from any client of that router without mDNS. Leave that on (default) and note it as the fallback if .local ever fails.

WAN

  • [ ] WAN as DHCP client. Nothing else: no VPN client, no AdGuard, no DNS override, no "Internet kill switch", no parental control.
  • [ ] Double NAT (ours behind NT's) is expected and fine for everything we do.

System

  • [ ] Auto-update off. Scheduled reboot off. No maintenance of any kind for the duration.
  • [ ] Admin password set and written on the router with tape; the same on all four.
  • [ ] Export the configuration (System → Backup) and load all three exports onto the spare and onto the instructor's USB stick.
  • [ ] Label each router with its zone colour, both sides, large.

2.3 Placement

  • One router per zone area, at the zone's centre, on a stand about 2 m up, antennas vertical. Boards sit at table height among people, laptops and water glasses; height beats power for even coverage.
  • Mains power at each position (the Flints are not PoE-powered). Bring a 10 m extension lead and gaffer tape per router.
  • Ethernet from NT's port to each router: bring a 50 m cable per router in case the ports are all in one corner.
  • The spare stays with the instructor kit, powered, on the bench, WAN unplugged.

2.4 Kit handout follows the router

The zone is compiled into each board, so the zone is now spatial as well as logical: a red team must sit where the red router is. Practically:

  • Divide the floor into three areas before the room opens. Signs in each colour.
  • Hand red kits out only in the red area, and so on. The kit colour is the check.
  • All three routers will be audible from anywhere in a ballroom, so a red kit in the green area still works, only with a weaker signal. "Stay in your zone" is about signal quality, not connectivity.

3. Rules for participants: who joins which WiFi

This goes on the team card and is said aloud at the start of Session 1.

Device Joins Why
The team's Home Assistant laptop (the one running the VM) The team's zone 5 GHz SSID, farm-laptop-<colour> — and nothing else It must find the team's boards by hostname; that only works on the zone network
One phone per team (for the dashboard) The team's zone 5 GHz SSID Same reason; the dashboard is on the laptop, not on the internet
Every other laptop and phone NT's internet WiFi Worksheet site and web.esphome.io are online; nothing else is needed
The two boards Nothing to do: the zone 2.4 GHz SSID is compiled in

The Home Assistant laptop must not have NT's WiFi saved. If it does, Windows and macOS will hop to it whenever the zone signal dips, Home Assistant loses its boards, and nothing on screen says why. Forget the NT network on that laptop, or never join it.

Each zone SSID has room for the HA laptop and one phone per team only. If a whole team of four joins the zone WiFi with every device, the router carries ~200 clients instead of ~140; it may cope, but the bench test (section 4) does not cover it.

Instructor kit and stage. The instructor kit laptop joins its own kit's zone SSID like any team, and gets internet through that router for the Session 3 Home Assistant Cloud demo. The presentation laptop uses NT's fourth wired port, or NT's WiFi.

4. Acceptance test

Run during the setup window, before anything is handed out. Tests 1–4 need only NT's three ports to be live; the rest need our routers in position. All must pass.

# Test Pass criterion
1 Each router's WAN, plugged into its NT port Gets a lease; router's own diagnostics reach the internet — no portal, no MAC auth (1.1)
2 Laptop on farm-laptop-red opens https://web.esphome.io and the worksheet site Both load — internet through the router
3 Laptop on NT's WiFi opens the same two sites Both load — NT WiFi works for the other three members
4 WiFi scanner on a laptop in each zone area NT's 5 GHz radios are not on 36, 44 or 149; no 2.4 GHz network other than our three (1.2, 1.3)
5 Board on farm-iot-red, laptop on farm-laptop-red: ping <boardname>.local Resolves and replies — cross-band mDNS inside a zone
6 Same ping from inside the laptop's bridged VM Resolves and replies — multicast reaches the VM, two MACs behind one client
7 Same laptop: http://<boardname>/ (no .local) Loads — the router's DNS fallback works
8 Phone on farm-laptop-red opens http://<VM-ip> Dashboard loads — client-to-client, port 80
9 Laptop on farm-laptop-green pings the red board by name; then dns-sd -B _esphome._tcp Fails; only green boards listed — zones isolated (should be true by construction; proves nothing is bridged by mistake)
10 Laptop on NT's WiFi opens http://<VM-ip> Fails — documents the rule in section 3 for anyone who doubts it
11 Laptop connected to a board, idle 30 minutes Connection still live
12 20–30 boards on one router, then power-cycle that router All associate and get leases; all rejoin within two minutes of the restart
13 Spare swap drill: unplug the red router, plug the spare in, restore the red export Boards and laptop reconnect; under five minutes end to end
14 Laptop with both farm-laptop-red and NT's WiFi saved, walk to the far side of the room Note what it does. This is the failure the team-card rule prevents; the point is to have seen it

What this cannot test. 20–30 boards is about a third of one router's 2.4 GHz load and none of its 5 GHz load. It validates configuration. Router capacity at ~140 clients is the one open question in this design (section 5) and is what the office bench test before the event is for: as many boards, laptops and phones as can be borrowed, on one router, with the power-cycle.

6. Why this replaced the VLAN design

The VLAN design (NETWORK-REQUIREMENTS.md) asked the provider to build three VLANs, broadcast six SSIDs on every AP, forward mDNS inside each VLAN and not between them, tolerate two MACs per client and hold long DHCP leases. Every one of those is off by default on managed enterprise WiFi, the acceptance test existed to catch them, and one wrong controller setting (an mDNS reflector turned on "helpfully") would have silently collapsed the zoning into a flat 500-client multicast domain.

With three routers we own:

  • Zone isolation is physical. Separate subnets behind separate NATs; nothing to reflect.
  • The 2.4 GHz plan is exactly the ideal one: three radios on 1 / 6 / 11, one per zone, and with NT's 2.4 GHz off they are alone in the band.
  • mDNS across bands works by default on an OpenWrt bridge; there is no controller feature to turn off.
  • NT's job shrinks to the thing enterprise WiFi is good at: internet for 600 laptops and phones, plus three cable ports.
  • The firmware does not change. Same SSIDs, same password, same zone build.

What it costs: zones become spatial (kits handed out by area), a team's HA laptop must stay off the NT WiFi, and one consumer router per zone carries a client count nobody publishes a figure for. Section 5 covers each.

Dropped from the old spec, no longer needed anywhere: VLAN IDs, per-SSID isolation settings, mDNS-reflector prohibition, multicast-to-unicast conversion, association-rate limits, the "fallback flat subnet" (section 6 of the old document) and outbound 8883, since the Session 3 remote-access demo is Home Assistant Cloud over 443 and MQTT left the worksheet.
